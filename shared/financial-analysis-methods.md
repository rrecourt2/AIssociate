# Financial analysis methods

The institution's definitions and approved spread govern; preserve reported accounting and contractual definitions.

## Comparable movements

For restatements, acquisitions/disposals, accounting changes or FX effects, distinguish reported and like-for-like comparisons. Use explained restated comparatives; do not fabricate missing like-for-like inputs.

Zero/negative baselines can make growth percentages undefined or misleading; show absolute movements instead. Distinguish percentage-point margin changes from relative changes. Retain reconciliation precision and round for presentation.

Do not sum percentage-point effects computed against different revenue bases. A cost divided by current revenue gives its mechanical effect on the current margin, not automatically its contribution to the year-on-year margin bridge.

## Driver evidence

Use disclosed volume/price/mix/perimeter/FX evidence. Volume and price changes can interact; adding their percentages does not give a precise bridge. For homogeneous quantity/price data, one consistent bridge is prior-price volume effect plus current-volume price effect. Mixed products, acquisitions and currency changes need an explicit basis and retained unexplained effects.

Qualitative commentary may support an attributed explanation without quantifying its contribution. Quantify only evidenced effects; a residual is not proof of a favoured cause.

## Earnings quality

Keep reported earnings visible. For proposed adjustments retain source amount, accounting location, recurrence, business rationale, cash/non-cash character and relevant covenant treatment. Avoid double counting exclusions already in imported EBITDA. Reconcile lease treatment, capitalised costs and accounting changes before comparing debt/EBITDA ratios.

A non-cash inventory write-down may reflect a continuing commercial problem; its reversal does not establish future cash receipts. Distinguish management-adjusted, analyst-proposed and institution-accepted measures.

## Cash conversion

Follow the actual cash-flow statement's classifications for taxes, interest, non-cash items and working capital. Reconcile opening/closing cash and financing flows without balancing plugs.

Acquisitions, FX, reclassifications and non-cash movements can make balance-sheet working-capital deltas differ from cash flows. Use disclosed cash-flow adjustments before treating the full delta as cash.

Working-capital days depend on numerator/denominator, average/closing balances and period length. Record the method; avoid comparisons across definitions or mechanical annualisation of seasonal interims. Factoring, supplier finance, overdue suppliers and customer advances can temporarily improve operating cash while creating obligations.
