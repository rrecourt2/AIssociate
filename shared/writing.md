# Writing investment documents

Use the brief's purpose, author perspective, audience and length. Approved examples of the same document type set voice, flow and detail; the template sets required content. Use one primary example, others only where helpful. Examples supply style, never deal facts. Report missing references rather than claiming house-style calibration.

| Output | Focus |
|---|---|
| Opportunity scan, typically two pages | Opportunity, fit, alternatives, material unknowns and whether to investigate further |
| Clearance in Principle (CIP) | Preliminary case, proposed direction and work or decisions needed before fuller approval |
| Term Sheet | Supplied template; precise terms, their status and proposed deviations |
| Contracting | Proposed clauses/redlines, departures from agreed terms and outstanding conditions or obligations; specialists clear |
| Financial Proposal (FP) | Defensible approval case, terms, risks, mitigants and residual exposure |
| External Investment Memorandum | Investment case for the named issuer and recipients, using cleared information and material qualifications |

These are purposes, not compulsory stages. Match depth to the next decision. Early gaps can remain explicit; later work should resolve them, record a justified deferral or identify a blocker. Do not infer approval or close a gap merely because the document is finished.

Use **ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical English)** selectively when explaining unfamiliar concepts or mechanisms. The examples always take priority. Do not apply it across the paper or impose a word list, sentence limit or compliance target. Preserve natural connections, familiar institutional wording and necessary qualifications.

Where a recommendation is intended, write the strongest defensible case for it and surface contrary evidence. Otherwise explore the question without inventing a recommendation. Explain observations, material risks, mitigants and residual exposure where relevant, not as compulsory headings. Describe the business and financial position in their natural order.

Explain material financial movements, supported drivers and their implications for cash and repayment. Distinguish existing protections from proposed conditions and assumptions. Do not force an acceptable conclusion where the evidence leaves a material question open.

Keep internal actions and fuller reasoning in Analysis, client evidence requests in the DDQ, and material qualifications in the document. Rewrite specialist inputs for the audience. Use annexes only where useful; summarise decisive results in the body. Before external circulation, exclude internal-only notes, comments, revisions, metadata and links. Retain review markup intended for those recipients and material qualifications.

Review whole sections for flow, density, repetition and fit beside the examples. Preserve attribution, uncertainty, scope and meaning during edits. Unsupported claims return for substantive review; smoother wording is not stronger evidence.
