# Drafting the scan, the CIP and the FP

You draft these in Word with your own tools. The tool gives you the material.

## Before you draft

1. Run `status --all` and `check`. Repair what `check` reports.
2. Read the Deal page, the contents page and the pages the document covers.
3. Ask the officer for the institution's template if you do not have it. Follow its headings.

## What each document is

- **A scan** says what the deal is and whether it fits. It may rest on what the client said. Say so.
- **A CIP** describes the deal and lists the claims that still need testing. Take them from `status` (the claims that are not tested, or that rest on someone's own word), from "Assumptions and open points" on the pages, and from the checks that are open or thin.
- **An FP** is driven by conclusions on risk. Go through the pillars. For each claim that matters: what the lender relies on, what tested it, what the evidence is and how strong, and for a claim that is challenged what mitigates it or which action is agreed. Take the evidence from the facts. A claim that is challenged, contested or thinly supported is said to be so.

## Rules

- Condense. The wiki holds the wider analysis. The document holds what the reader needs to decide.
- Take every number from a fact, with its unit, date and company. Do not retype a number from memory.
- Say who said what. "The CFO said" is not "the accounts show".
- State what is not known.
- Save the draft in `Internal/outputs`. When the officer corrects the draft, correct the fact and the page as well.
